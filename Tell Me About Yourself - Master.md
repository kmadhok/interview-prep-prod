# Tell Me About Yourself — Master

**Purpose.** One canonical TMAY spine plus 3 archetype variants. When a new role enters the pipeline, copy the closest variant into the role folder as `Tell Me About Yourself - Cue Card.md` and tune the closing "why this role" line.

**The spine doesn't change.** Five beats, ~90 seconds spoken, ends on a clean closing line that cues the interviewer to ask the follow-up I want.

---

## The spine (role-agnostic)

1. **Present.** Sr Data Analyst at Walmart Data Ventures supporting Customer Perception — point of contact for ~30 stakeholders, business + technical, covering analytics, stewardship, readiness.
2. **The shift.** Intake volume was more than one person could keep up with. Started building — probabilistic and deterministic systems — to absorb it.
3. **What grew out of it.** Power BI dashboards → Python pipelines → Jira resolution agent (400+ drafted stakeholder responses without anyone realizing it wasn't all me) → autonomous data analyst → self-growing semantic layer.
4. **Recognition.** Making a Difference Award earlier this year for 2025 work.
5. **Why this role.** [Variant-specific closing line — see below.]

---

## How to use this file

- Practice the **full version** of the chosen variant out loud until the **beat order** is automatic.
- In the interview, glance only at the bolded hook line — that's the thing you most want planted.
- If you forget your place, the 5 section headers (Present → Shift → Built → Recognition → Why) are your recovery map.
- **End cleanly, then stop.** Silence is the cue for the interviewer to ask their follow-up.
- The strongest hook on every variant is the **Jira agent line** — it almost always pulls the first follow-up. Make sure it lands clearly.

# Variant A — Agent Builder / platform engineering

**Use for:** Walmart Principal SWE Agent Builder. Any role where the panel is engineers evaluating agent system design.

**Closing line:** *"I've been doing this scoped to one team — the role makes it possible to do it at the scale Walmart actually operates at, and that's the version of this work I want to be doing."*

**Strongest hook to plant:** Jira agent (technical depth + leverage flywheel).

**Full version:**

> Today I'm a Senior Data Analyst at Walmart Data Ventures supporting Customer Perception — I'm the point of contact for about 30 people across business and technical teams who come to me with analytics, stewardship, and readiness questions. Pretty quickly I realized the volume of that intake wasn't something one person could keep up with, so I started building — both probabilistic and deterministic systems — to absorb it. That's grown into Power BI dashboards, Python pipelines, **a Jira resolution agent that's drafted 400+ stakeholder responses without anyone realizing the answers aren't all coming from me**, an autonomous data analyst, and a self-growing semantic layer that refines its own definitions over time. Earlier this year that work earned me a Making a Difference Award for what I shipped in 2025. What pulls me toward Agent Builder is that I've been doing this scoped to one team — the role makes it possible to do it at the scale Walmart actually operates at, and that's the version of this work I want to be doing.

**Hooks ranked by likelihood of follow-up:**

1. Jira agent — strongest line.
2. Self-growing semantic layer.
3. "Scoped to one team" → "you're already doing this, why move?"
4. Autonomous data analyst.

# Variant B — Consulting / product builder (BCG X archetype)

**Use for:** BCG X AI Factory Product Builder. Roles where the panel wants someone who can sit with consultants, break down delivery workflows, and ship things consultants adopt.

**Closing line:** *"That's exactly the shape of AI Factory work — finding the high-leverage workflows inside an organization and building the assets that scale across them. I want to do that with consulting teams, not just one analytics function."*

**Strongest hook to plant:** Jira agent (same flywheel framing) *and* productized Wibey skills (proves I can ship things others adopt through formal review).

**What changes from Variant A:**

- "Customer Perception" stays, but I lean harder on "I built things real business teams adopted" rather than "I built agents."
- Foreground the *productized Wibey skills* line — first-of-kind across DV, cleared Product + DS partnership review. That story translates 1:1 to "ship reusable assets that scale across the firm."
- Add a line about the FTI capstone — RAG generator adopted into a consulting firm's actual workflow, won Best in Show. That's signal directly relevant to BCG X.

**Full version:**

> Today I'm a Senior Data Analyst at Walmart Data Ventures, supporting Customer Perception — I sit at the intersection of business and technical teams, point of contact for about 30 stakeholders on analytics, stewardship, and data readiness. The intake volume on that role was more than one person could keep up with, so I started building — probabilistic and deterministic systems — to absorb it. That grew into a portfolio of things business teams actually use: Power BI dashboards, Python pipelines, **a Jira resolution agent that drafted 400+ stakeholder responses without anyone realizing it was an agent**, an autonomous data analyst that's the only AI skill in active use by business teams in Data Ventures, and a self-growing semantic layer co-built with Data Science and Product. Two of those shipped as **official productized skills inside Walmart's Claude Code fork — first-of-kind across Data Ventures**, cleared through formal Product and DS partnership review. Before Walmart, I shipped a RAG proposal generator into FTI Consulting's actual workflow — won Best in Show at UChicago. Earlier this year, the Walmart work earned a Making a Difference Award for 2025 impact. What pulls me toward AI Factory is that this is exactly the shape of work — finding the high-leverage workflows inside an organization and building the assets that scale across them. I want to do that with consulting teams, not just one analytics function.

**Hooks ranked by likelihood of follow-up:**

1. *"Productized inside Walmart's Claude Code fork — first-of-kind"* — opens the Claude Code experience question, which is **required** in the BCG X JD.
2. Jira agent.
3. FTI RAG generator (the consulting-coded artifact).
4. Self-growing semantic layer co-built with DS + Product (cross-functional signal).

# Variant C — Forward Deployed Engineer / client-facing (Snorkel + Deloitte archetype)

**Use for:** Snorkel FDE DaaS, Deloitte GPS Anthropic FDE. Roles where the panel wants someone who can drop into a client engagement, design eval/data workflows, build the technical foundations, and own the project end-to-end.

**Closing line:** *"That work has been internal so far — same team, same stakeholders, every week. The FDE role is the version where I'd be doing this across a portfolio of customers, with the same end-to-end ownership. That's the next shape of this work I want."*

**Strongest hook to plant:** HITL design on the Jira agent (eval/quality work is what FDE roles assess) + the engineering-rigor pipeline (S-A6) which signals I take production code seriously.

**What changes from Variant A/B:**

- Lead with the *intersection* framing — data science + data engineering + AI engineering + ops. Snorkel's JD calls this out verbatim.
- Foreground **HITL** specifically — both Snorkel and Deloitte want someone who can design human-in-the-loop pipelines that actually work.
- Add **the donation experiment (S-U1)** as the eval/causal-design signal — it's the cleanest proof I can design measurement, not just ship.
- Add **the FTI capstone** (client-facing, adopted by a real org) as the FDE-shaped artifact.

**Full version:**

> Today I'm a Senior Data Analyst at Walmart Data Ventures supporting Customer Perception — but the actual work sits at the intersection of data science, data engineering, and AI engineering. I'm the point of contact for about 30 stakeholders across business and technical teams, and the volume of that intake forced me to start building. What grew out of it is a portfolio of agents: **a Jira resolution agent where I deliberately kept myself as the human in the loop — agent drafts, I review and post, stakeholders never see the agent**; an autonomous data analyst that publishes its own analyses on a 740K-panelist dataset with analyst review at the publish gate; a 4-layer ML pipeline that replaced weeks of manual schema mapping with 45 seconds of typed, tested code; and a self-growing semantic layer where disagreements between a deterministic backend and an agent backend become labeled training data for new semantic-layer entries. Before Walmart I designed a 500+ participant LLM persuasion experiment with controlled persona conditions at UChicago, and shipped a RAG proposal generator into FTI Consulting's workflow that won Best in Show. Earlier this year the Walmart work earned a Making a Difference Award. That work has been internal so far — same team, same stakeholders, every week. The FDE role is the version where I'd be doing this across a portfolio of customers, with the same end-to-end ownership. That's the next shape of this work I want.

**Hooks ranked by likelihood of follow-up:**

1. *"Deliberately kept myself as the human in the loop"* — HITL is the FDE rubric.
2. *"Disagreements between a deterministic backend and an agent backend become labeled training data"* — direct Snorkel-shaped framing.
3. ML pipeline with typing + tests — engineering-rigor signal.
4. FTI capstone — client-facing artifact.

# Variant tuning checklist

Before delivering a TMAY in any interview, run through:

- [ ] Closing line names *this* role and *why now*, not a generic "I want to keep growing." Specific > generic every time.
- [ ] One hook line is bolded in your head — the thing you want the interviewer to pull on first.
- [ ] You can deliver it in ~90 seconds. If it's running 2+ min, cut a beat.
- [ ] You're not reciting the JD back at them. The "Why this role" line should be a *connection*, not a summary.
- [ ] You're not bashing your current role. The move is *toward* something, not *away* from Walmart.
- [ ] You're foregrounding ownership and business outcome, not the tech stack.

# Notes

- The Walmart "scoped to one team → at Walmart scale" framing works **only for the Walmart Agent Builder role** (it's internal mobility, same company). For external roles, swap to "internal work → portfolio of customers / consulting teams / firm-wide adoption" depending on the variant.
- The "Making a Difference Award" line is universal — keep it. It's recent, concrete, third-party validation.
- If a panel is short (≤30 min), cut the recognition beat — keep Present → Shift → Built → Why. Lose the award before you lose the hook.
- If the panel is engineering-heavy and asks for a *technical* TMAY, run Variant A but swap beat 3 to lead with architecture: "context layer + MCP → orchestrator → individual agents on top."
