## Appendix — Walmart coaching story scaffold

**What it needs:** a *person who could do more afterward* — not a tool that got used. Two people-managers on the panel weight this; Michael Hawkins' lens is people + AI.

**90-sec structure:** who + what they were stuck on → your move (transfer capability: paired / taught a pattern / built reusable thing AND showed them to extend it) → the judgment (raise the team's floor vs. be the bottleneck) → outcome (they do it independently / pattern spread to N analysts; honest numbers) → tie to role ("the part I'm drawn to — a mandate to do this on purpose, not on the side of my desk").

**Candidate angles — pick the one with a real name:**
- First-analyst Wibey skills (S-A2): walked a specific analyst through using/extending a skill instead of filing a ticket.
- Taught someone to build their first agent/skill — they came to you, you paired, they shipped.
- Handed the reusable substrate (golden-set eval / context layer) to an analyst as a pattern they extended — "gave the pattern, not the answer."
- Onboarded/unblocked a teammate on the 67-table schema or Jira workflow — name + before/after.

**Fill-in draft:**
> "[Name] was [doing X manually / stuck on Y]. I could've just done it for them, but the whole team had the same gap. So I [paired on building their first Wibey skill / walked them through the reusable context-layer pattern] — the point was they could do it themselves after, not depend on me. [Now they run it independently / N analysts picked up the pattern]. That's deliberately how I work — I'd rather hand the team a substrate they extend than a one-off they consume. It's also the part of this role I'm most drawn to: doing it with a mandate instead of on the side of my desk."

**Fallback (no named person):** lead with the reusable-patterns/Wibey-skills framing, but acknowledge it's pattern-level and offer the smallest concrete moment you have.
