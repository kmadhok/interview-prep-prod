# Feedback Request — Panel (Slack DM)

**Why Slack, not email.** You're internal, you already sent Slack thank-yous, and the thread is warm. Opening a new email channel for the feedback ask resets the formality and reads as more transactional. DM into the existing thank-you thread (or a fresh DM if the thank-you was in a channel) and keep it short.

**Timing.** Within 2–3 days of the rejection. Same-day looks reactive; past a week the panel debrief is no longer fresh in anyone's head.

**Send to all three, but stagger by ~24 hours.** They sit close enough that simultaneous asks will surface — staggering looks intentional. Order matters: start with the panelist whose segment you think was the weakest, because their feedback is the highest-information. Default order based on the prep breakdown:

1. **Michael Ferrell** first — he was closest to the data/implementation lane, where the surprise project walkthrough was hardest to land cold.
2. **Nathan Oswalt** next — peer, automation roots, most likely to give a candid practitioner read.
3. **Navin Kasa** last — strategy/product seat; his feedback will be more about narrative + scope framing than execution.

**Don't ask the HM in the same wave.** HM gets its own message (see `Feedback Request - HM.md`) — different ask, different framing.

---

## Universal structure

Three beats, ~60 words:

1. Close the loop: "heard the decision didn't land my way, fine, wanted to follow up."
2. One specific thing you'd like their read on — name it yourself, don't ask "any feedback."
3. Make it easy to say no.

## What you're really doing

You're not begging for feedback — you're banking it. The actual ROI of this exercise isn't a re-interview; it's three pieces of intel that calibrate your next 5–10 panels. Specifically:

- **Was the surprise-presentation pivot the swing factor, or was it something else?** Critical to know, because if "something else" you've been over-indexing on the wrong fix.
- **Did the project narrative land at the right level of abstraction for each lane** (Michael = implementation, Nathan = execution, Navin = strategy)? That's a transferable skill check.
- **How does the panel describe the gap to themselves?** The exact words they use are gold for future answers.

## What NOT to do

- Don't ask for a re-interview, referral to another team, or LinkedIn endorsement in this message. Separate ask, separate moment.
- Don't relitigate the brief. The "wasn't in the prep brief" line in Michael's message is the only acceptable place to even reference it, and even there it's framed as your problem to solve, not theirs.
- Don't follow up if they don't reply. Silence is a complete "no" and respecting that protects the working relationship — you still see these people in Walmart channels.
- Don't BCC the recruiter. This is panelist-to-panelist; the recruiter being a fly on the wall changes how they answer.

## If they reply with substance

Reply within 24 hours. Two sentences: name the specific thing they flagged, tell them what you're going to do differently. No "I'll prove you wrong" — just "here's what I'm taking away." That's the response that gets remembered next time their team has a req.
