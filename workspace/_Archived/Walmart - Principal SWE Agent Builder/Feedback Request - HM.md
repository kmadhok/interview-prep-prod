# Feedback Request — Hiring Manager

**This is the most important of the four messages.** The HM saw the full debrief, weighted the panel inputs, and made the call. Their feedback is *strictly* more informative than any single panelist's.

**You already have a pre-panel conversation with them as warm ground.** Reference it lightly — it signals you remember the substance of what they said the team needed, and frames the feedback ask as "calibrating against the bar you described to me," not "tell me what I did wrong."

**Channel.** Slack DM. You already have the working relationship; email is heavier than this conversation needs to be.

**Timing.** Day 1–2 after the rejection. The HM is the one person whose memory of you is most likely to be displaced by the next round of candidates within a week.

**Tone.** Slightly more direct than the panelist asks. The HM signed off on hiring someone else — they owe you (in the loose social-debt sense) a candid line, and they know it. Asking for a specific thing makes it easy for them to deliver.

---

## The message

> Hey [HM first name] — appreciate you letting me know. No hard feelings; closing it out cleanly.
>
> One ask while the debrief is fresh: from your side, was the swing factor the project walkthrough segment, or was the gap somewhere else? When you and I talked before the panel you described the bar as [refer to the specific phrase or capability they used — e.g., "someone who's already shipped agents in production, not someone who could learn to," or "stakeholder fluency on top of the build chops"] — and I'd like to know whether I was off the mark on that bar specifically, or whether the issue was somewhere I haven't identified yet.
>
> Even one line is useful. No pressure if it's not something you can share.
>
> Thanks for the shot at this one.
>
> Kanu

---

## Why this version works

**The pre-panel call-back.** Most rejected candidates ask "any feedback?" and the HM defaults to a generic safe answer. By referencing the specific phrase the HM used to describe the bar in your pre-panel chat, you're forcing the answer to be *measured against their own stated criterion*. That's much harder to deflect with a generic line.

**The binary frame.** "Project walkthrough vs. something else" is a fork the HM can answer in two words. Open-ended "what could I improve" is a paragraph they have to write. Make it cheap to respond.

**The acknowledgment that you've already identified the project walkthrough as a likely issue.** This is the move that makes the HM more likely to be honest: if you've already done the self-assessment work, they're not breaking news to you, they're confirming or correcting a hypothesis. That feels less like delivering bad feedback and more like a peer conversation.

---

## Before you send — fill in the bar phrase

Pull from memory or notes:

- What did the HM say the team was struggling with that this hire would solve?
- Did they describe the role in terms of *execution* (someone who ships), *judgment* (someone who picks the right thing to build), or *stakeholder fluency* (someone who can win over the business teams)?
- Was there a specific gap they named on the current team that this role was meant to close?

That phrase or capability goes into the `[refer to the specific phrase...]` bracket. If you don't remember a specific phrase, substitute a paraphrase — but try hard to surface something concrete. Generic ("the bar you described") is much weaker than specific ("the bar around shipping production agents end-to-end vs. proof-of-concept work").

---

## If the HM replies with substance

This is the highest-value reply you'll get. Treat it accordingly:

1. **Reply within 12 hours**, not 24. The HM is busy and you want to be on the inside of the response window.
2. **Two beats, ~50 words.** Name the specific gap they flagged. Say what you're going to do about it in concrete terms (a project you'll ship, a skill you'll build, an approach you'll change). Skip the apologetic tone.
3. **Don't ask about future reqs in the same message.** Wait 4–6 weeks, then send a separate note: "Hey, you mentioned X — wanted to flag that I've now shipped Y. If there's a future Agent Builder seat where that closes the gap you flagged, I'd want to know."

That two-message pattern is how a rejection becomes a future offer. The feedback ask is just the first move.
