# 7-Day Interview Prep Schedule

**Role:** Walmart Principal Software Engineer — Agent Builder
**Daily budget:** 1–2 hours
**Principle:** Story practice front-loaded. Business framing reps and agent design walkthroughs in the middle. Last day = buffer + sleep.

> **Format note (confirmed by a team insider, 2026-05-11):** No leetcode. The interview is business/capability and end-to-end problem-solving — every story should be told business-problem-first, with quantified outcomes. Reweight accordingly. Old guidance about LeetCode warm-ups has been removed.

---

## Day 1 — Orient & lock the framing (90 min)

The point of today is to read everything once, decide which stories you're actually going to lead with, and pick your relocation answer. No practice yet.

- [ ] Read the full prep doc end to end (45 min)
- [ ] Re-read the JD slowly. Highlight verbs you can map to a project (15 min)
- [ ] Pick the **4–6 STAR stories** you'll lead with from Section 7. Mark them in the doc (15 min)
- [ ] Decide your **relocation answer** (Bentonville vs Chicago). Write the one-sentence version. Don't decide in the room (15 min)

**End-of-day check:** Can you list your 4–6 stories from memory? If not, write them on a sticky note and put it on your monitor.

---

## Day 2 — Pitch + your 3 lead stories, told business-first (90 min)

Today is voice work. Practice out loud, not in your head. Reading silently is the most common way to fool yourself into thinking you're prepared.

For each story, force yourself to open with the **business problem and its cost** — not the tech stack. End every story with the **quantified outcome** (dollars, hours, %, count). This is the register the panel is listening for.

- [ ] Practice your **60-second opener** out loud, 5× (20 min)
- [ ] Record yourself doing the opener once on your phone. Listen back. Trim what feels stiff (10 min)
- [ ] Practice your **3 lead stories at 90 seconds each**, 3× per story — business-problem-first, quantified-outcome-last (45 min)
- [ ] Time yourself on each. If you're over 100 seconds, cut, don't speed up (15 min)

**End-of-day check:** Can you tell each story in 90 seconds, opening with the problem in business terms and closing with a number, without notes?

---

## Day 3 — Failure story + 3 more stories (90 min)

The failure story is the most common gap in candidate prep. Don't skip this day.

- [ ] **Find your real failure story** (Story 10 prompt in the doc). Write it out STAR-style. 30 minutes minimum (30 min)
- [ ] Practice the failure story out loud 3× (15 min)
- [ ] Practice 3 backup stories from the bank at 90 seconds (45 min)

**End-of-day check:** If they ask "tell me about a time you failed," do you have a 90-second answer that doesn't feel rehearsed?

---

## Day 4 — End-to-end agent design walkthroughs (90–120 min)

This is the centerpiece day now. The interview tests whether you can take a vague business problem and walk a panel through how you'd scope, design, deploy, and measure an agent for it — out loud, on the fly, in 5 minutes. That capability is exactly what the format is checking for.

- [ ] Skim Anthropic's **"Building effective agents"** post — just enough to use the vocabulary (20 min)
- [ ] Skim the **MCP architecture overview** (10 min)
- [ ] Sketch your **Data Ventures agent architecture** from memory on paper. Three minutes max. Then sketch it again (15 min)
- [ ] **Drill "design an agent for X" out loud — 3 prompts, 7 minutes each.** Pick from: "inventory exception handling," "vendor onboarding," "merchandising price-change review," "store-level out-of-stock detection," "supplier scorecard generation." For each one, walk through: (1) what's broken today and the cost of it, (2) what the agent does, (3) which APIs/triggers/data sources it chains, (4) the human-in-the-loop / failure mode handling, (5) how you'd measure success in dollars or hours saved (45 min)
- [ ] **Prioritization reps**: pick one of your design prompts. Out loud, justify why you'd build *this* agent before two other candidates. Use Pareto / leverage / reversibility as your filters (15 min)
- [ ] **Technical-register check**: practice crisp 30-second answers to "how would you handle [API rate-limiting / hallucinations / a downstream system going down / a human approver who's on PTO]" (15 min)

**End-of-day check:** Pick a fresh prompt you haven't drilled. Can you walk through all 5 steps above in 5 minutes without notes?

---

## Day 5 — Curveballs, per-panelist questions, Walmart news (90 min)

Today is the texture work — the non-content things that make you sound prepared instead of generic.

- [ ] Practice the harder questions out loud (45 min)
  - Why this role / why now / why not just stay at Data Ventures
  - What concerns do you have about this role
  - How would you decide which agent to build first
  - Walk me through your stack
- [ ] Read panelist briefs again. Finalize **2 questions per panelist** — different for each (20 min)
- [ ] **Walmart news scan**: search "Walmart Sparky," "Walmart agent platform," "Walmart merchandising AI" — last 60 days. Note one current reference you can drop (20 min)
- [ ] Have the conversation with your **current manager** if you haven't yet. Or schedule it for tomorrow morning (5 min)

**End-of-day check:** Two specific questions written down for each panelist?

---

## Day 6 — Full mock interview (90–120 min)

This is the most uncomfortable day, which is why it's the highest leverage. If you have a friend who can play interviewer, ask them. If not, do it solo: read each question out loud, then answer out loud while standing up.

- [ ] **Full mock interview, 60 min straight.** Pull questions from Section 7. Answer out loud. Time yourself. No re-reading allowed (60 min)
- [ ] Identify your weakest 2–3 answers from the mock (10 min)
- [ ] Redo just those answers (20 min)
- [ ] Re-read the prep doc's "If you only have 30 minutes" callout (5 min)

**End-of-day check:** Are there any questions where you're still hesitating mid-answer? Practice those one more time.

---

## Day 7 — Day before: light review only (45 min, then stop)

Resist the urge to cram. The goal is rest and sharpness, not new content.

- [ ] Re-read your 60-second pitch once (5 min)
- [ ] Re-read your 3 lead stories once (15 min)
- [ ] Re-read the panelist briefs once (15 min)
- [ ] Confirm logistics: time, link/address, who you'll meet first (5 min)
- [ ] Lay out two questions per panelist on a single page or note (5 min)
- [ ] **Stop prepping by 7pm.** Eat well, sleep well

**End-of-day check:** Phone charged, link confirmed, alarm set.

---

## Day of — keep it light

- [ ] Re-read panelist briefs in the morning (10 min)
- [ ] Have your resume open in another tab. Have the JD open in another
- [ ] Drink water before the call
- [ ] Pause before answering. Three seconds and a good answer beats one second and a rushed one
- [ ] Remember: you've already done this job. Talk like a peer, not a candidate

---

## After the interview

- [ ] Within 24 hours, send each panelist a brief, **personalized thank-you note**. Reference one specific thing they said
- [ ] Write down what they actually asked. If you go to a next round, the next prep starts from this list

---

## What to skip

- Reading the prep doc more than once total
- Memorizing answers verbatim
- **LeetCode and DSA prep — confirmed not on this interview**
- System-design deep dives (distributed systems trivia, CAP theorem, etc.) — wrong register
- Generic "tell me about a time…" practice — practice your specific stories
- Cramming the day before
- Worrying about questions you haven't been asked yet

---

## If you fall behind

Skip in this order: Day 5 Walmart news → Day 3 backup stories → Day 2 pitch repetitions → Day 4 "Building effective agents" reading (you already know enough). **Never skip:** the failure story (Day 3), the Day 4 design-an-agent drills, the mock interview (Day 6), or sleep (Day 7).
