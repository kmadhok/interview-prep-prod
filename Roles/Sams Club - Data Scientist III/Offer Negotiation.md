**Two takeaways to use:**

## The recruiter's own range math (your strongest card)

The recruiter told you they **typically hire 40–50% into the range**, and the range for this role is **$90k–$180k**.

- Range width $90k; midpoint **$135k**; each 10% of range = $9k.
- "40–50% into the range" = **$126,000 – $135,000** — their *own* stated typical hiring zone.
- Your offer of **$115k sits at just 28% of the range** — *below* that zone by $11–20k.
- A **$130k** ask = **44% of range** → dead center of where he said they hire.
- **$135k** = the midpoint (50%) → still just "middle of the range," not a reach.

This reframes the whole negotiation: you're not asking them to stretch, you're asking them to **honor the hiring policy the recruiter just described.** Quote it back to him.

## The read

Your **total comp (~$182k) is actually at/above the levels.fyi L3 US average (~$180k)** — but *only because your equity is rich*. Your $50k/yr stock is **~$18k above** the market L3 grant (~$32k). That's a genuine strength; don't trade it away.

The soft spot is **base**:
- **$18,900 below** levels.fyi L3 US base ($133.9k)
- **$5,000 below** the H1B median ($120k)
- **below** the real $130–135k Walmart DS III base filings

**Why base is the right lever:** it's guaranteed cash (no vesting, no market risk), it's the multiplier for your 15% bonus, and merit raises are a % of base — so it compounds. Each +$10k base ≈ +$11.5k/yr in cash+bonus, every year. Equity is back-loaded and conditional.

## Reading the recruiter's "40–50% of the range" line

The recruiter said they typically hire **40–50% into the range** (around midpoint). Two takeaways:

- **It's a soft anchor.** Saying "this is where we normally land people" is meant to make $115k feel standard and pre-empt your push. It's a framing, not a hard rule.
- **It reveals the band.** If $115k ≈ the 40–50% point and the H1B median is ~$120k, their band is roughly **min ~$98k · mid ~$120k · max ~$145k** (consistent with real Walmart DS III filings reaching ~$154k).

**The reframe:** $130k isn't "above your band" — it's the **upper-third (~65–70%)** of the band they already have. Midpoint is for a *typical* hire. You're not typical: internal to the Walmart ecosystem, zero ramp time, HM came to *you*, and you're the one relocating. Those are exactly the factors that justify upper-third placement. So you're not asking them to break their structure — just to slot you where a strong, sought-after, relocating candidate belongs in it.

## Recommended ask

Equity is settled (~$50k, above market — leave it). Your two clean levers are **base** and **relocation**, and you can push both because you're not desperate and they're the ones asking you to move.

1. **Primary: raise base to $130,000** (stretch $135k, the midpoint). Fully defensible on two grounds: it's exactly **40–50% into the $90–180k range — the zone the recruiter said they typically hire into** — *and* it matches levels.fyi L3 median and real Walmart DS III filings. At $130k base + 15% + $50k stock → **~$199.5k total**.
2. **Relocation: get a real relo package, separate from base.** They're asking *you* to move to Bentonville, so this is their cost to carry, not yours. Ask for a **lump-sum relocation** (Walmart's standard is typically a tiered lump sum — push for the higher tier, ~$10–20k+) and/or **a sign-on bonus** to offset the move and the in-year disruption. This is the easiest "yes" for them because it's one-time money that doesn't touch the salary bands.
3. **Fallback if base is capped:** route the gap into a **larger sign-on bonus** or bump the **bonus target to 20%**.

**Walk-in target:** base **$128–133k**, hold $50k stock, 15% bonus, **plus** relocation + sign-on → **~$197–203k total comp** with the move fully covered. Anchor at $135k on base so you land in range.

## Leverage you actually have

- **You're not desperate.** You have a stable role at Walmart Data Ventures and active competing processes (BCG X, Walmart Principal Data Analyst). You can walk, and that's real leverage — use it calmly, not as a threat.
- **They're asking you to relocate.** That's the recruiter's burden to make worth your while — it justifies both a stronger base and a real relo/sign-on package.
- You're already inside the Walmart ecosystem — you ramp fast, know the tools (Wibey/Claude Code), zero learning curve. Lower risk for them.
- This was an **HM-initiated fast-track** — they came to you.

## Tactics

- Negotiate **base first, in one consolidated ask** — don't drip requests.
- Lead with enthusiasm for the role, then the data. "I'm genuinely excited — I want to make this easy to say yes to."
- Put the number in *their* mouth first if you can ("what's the flexibility on base?"), but be ready to anchor at **$135k**.
- Get the final offer **in writing** before you accept or decline anything.
- Don't apologize for asking. Silence after you state your number is fine — let them respond.

## Rebuttal cheat-sheet (for the call)

**"We typically hire at 40–50% of the range."**
> "Totally understand that's the norm for the role. My read is that midpoint is the right place for a typical hire — and I'd just point to a few things that put me above typical: I'm already inside the Walmart ecosystem so there's basically no ramp, you all came to me, and I'm the one taking on a relocation. $130k still sits inside your band, just in the upper-third where a candidate like that usually lands. Is there room to place me there?"

**"The equity makes up for the lower base."**
> "I really value the equity and I'm not looking to touch it. My one ask is on guaranteed base — it's what the bonus is calculated on and what raises build from, so getting it to market matters more to me than total headline number."

**"This is the best we can do on base."**
> "I hear you. If base is genuinely capped, can we close the gap another way — a sign-on to bridge year one, and a relocation package for the move? That keeps your band intact and still gets me comfortable saying yes."

**"Can you tell me what it would take?"** (they flip it to you)
> "$130k base, equity and bonus as offered, plus relocation support. Do that and I'm ready to sign."

**If they go quiet / push for a verbal yes now:**
> "I'm genuinely excited and I want to say yes — let me see the revised numbers in writing and I'll turn it around fast." (Don't accept or decline verbally on the spot.)
